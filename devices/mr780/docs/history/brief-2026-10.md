Brief: modifications to the Magnat MR 780 (initial draft)
Draft of 2026-10-28, put together from the SP103 project (alemser/sp103-mod). The idea is for it to become the docs/brief.md of a new repository (suggested name: mr780-display-proxy).

Goal (to be confirmed with the owner)
Repeat what was done on the SP103:

Read everything the amp's controller shows on the front display.
Keep the amp's state (volume, input, tones, etc.) and offer it over the network (Wi-Fi), to groove-rig, the phone and OceanO.
Change the display's text (input names, a welcome and, later, the track playing).
All reversible: display, controller and original functions remain intact. Step-by-step explanations.

Board chosen (owner's note, 2026-10-28)
This time the board is an Orange Pi Zero 2W or Raspberry Pi Zero 2W (Linux), not a Pico 2 W.

Consequences:

Linux is not real-time and has no PIO. Listening to a slow bus is possible (pigpio samples GPIO by DMA at ~1 MHz on the Raspberry; an SPI-like serial bus can go straight into the SPI peripheral). But the SP103's real-time write tricks (reacting in ~20 µs) are not possible on Linux.
If writing needs real time, the way out is a small helper (an RP2040/Pico just for the bus, connected to the Zero by UART/USB), with the Zero handling Wi-Fi, state and the page. Decide after the captures.
Advantages: normal Python (the sp103's MicroPython code ports easily), more memory, and the board could even run groove-rig or talk directly to OceanO.
The Raspberry Pi Zero 2W has more documentation and pigpio; the Orange Pi Zero 2W has more RAM. For this project, the Raspberry is the safer choice.
Power: the Zero needs 5 V and ~300–500 mA (much more than the Pico), and needs a clean Linux shutdown (or a read-only file system) so the card is not corrupted when the amp switches off.

What is already known about the MR 780 (source: Magnat's website)
Hybrid receiver: preamp with 2 ECC81 valves, transistor output, 2 x 100 W into 4 Ω.
Two-line display on the front. The type (HD44780 character LCD, VFD, dot matrix) is still unknown.
Inputs: 5 analogue, MM phono, 2 optical, 2 coaxial, USB audio (Wolfson DAC, 24/192), front 3.5 mm jack.
Bluetooth 4.0 aptX, DAB/DAB+ tuner and FM with RDS (radio text already appears on the display).
IR remote.
An important difference from the SP103: the MR 780 is much newer (2017) and has many more functions (DAB, BT, USB). The display is probably controlled by a modern microcontroller, and may be a VFD with a serial driver chip (for example from the PT6311/PT6312 family, SPI-like protocol), and not a parallel LCD like the SP103's. That changes the sniffer, but not the architecture.

Approach (same as the SP103)
Photos and measurements, without intruding on anything: open the lid, photograph the front board (both sides), the display, the chips near it (note the markings) and the flat cable between the boards.
Identify the display and the bus: with the multimeter (amp unplugged) and then with the logic analyzer (the same Pico 2 with the LogicAnalyzer firmware, the sp103's tools/logicanalyzer).
Captures: power-on, volume, each input, menus, DAB/RDS, Bluetooth. Decode and document in docs/descobertas-protocolo.md.
Screen reader → state (like amp_state.py), tested on the Mac with the captures.
Wi-Fi: /state, /events, page, /bus, updates over Wi-Fi (reuse web.py, ota.py, buslog.py).
Real listening inside the amp (Pico only listening).
Writing: decide, after the measurements, between tricks without cutting (as on the SP103) and interception.

Electrical constraints (inherited from the SP103, to be reviewed)
The Zero's GPIO is 3.3 V and does not tolerate 5 V (unlike the RP2350). If the bus is 5 V: a resistive divider or level shifter on each wire.
VFDs have high voltages on the filament/grid (tens of volts): measure before connecting any wire, and only on the logic side (data/clock/strobe).
Never touch the mains side. Wires only with the amp unplugged.

What the photos of the interior showed (2026-10-30)
Boards (all Kingboard, marked "MR780-..."):

Display: dot-matrix VFD, 2 lines x 16 characters (5x7), on its own board MR780-DISPLAY-D.DDB (2020-09-24). There is no visible driver chip on the display board, so the control must be in the glass itself (VFD with built-in controller, serial input) or on the main board. The display board has the VFD's high-voltage supply (Q1, DW1, D2–D4, 50 V electrolytics): do not connect anything there without measuring.
Display ↔ main connection: flat cable (FFC) of ~24 lines (CN8 on the main board). That is where the bus to listen to is.
Main board MR780_Main_PCB (2021-12-21): microcontroller U17 (LQFP-64, crystal beside it), XMOS (USB audio), WM8740 (DAC), Frontier Silicon module (DAB/FM), Bluetooth module. Connectors with D+5V, APD, SYS, VCC, DGND, ±12V, and "TO SMPS PCB" (POW/OF, DGND).
Standby supply MR780-SMPS-D (PN8370, 6 V relay that switches on the MT 900 toroidal). It is small: it cannot power a Pi Zero (300–500 mA).
Rear panel: USB "UPDATE ONLY" and SERVICE/NORMAL switch (firmware update of the controller).

Space, power and Wi-Fi
Metal case: not a problem. The SP103's Pico 2 W works on Wi-Fi inside a metal case (owner's correction, 2026-10-30).
The Pi Zero 2W is small (65 x 30 mm), but the free space is near the toroidal and the heatsinks (magnetic field and heat), and drawing 500 mA from the amp's 5 V is risky.

Revised plan
Pi Zero 2W outside the amp, in a small box behind it, with its own USB power supply. No new power supply inside the amp.
Inside the amp, only a small Pico 2 (or RP2040), stuck near the FFC, listening to the display bus (PIO, as on the sp103) and powered from D+5V (~25 mA). It sends the screens by UART over a thin 3-way cable (TX, GND and, if needed, RX) that goes out through the rear to the Zero.
The Zero does the rest: state, /state, /events, page, OceanO and groove-rig, in normal Python.
Simpler alternative, if Wi-Fi is not needed inside: the Zero can be up to 1–2 m away, the UART cable can take it.

Resumed: read only (2026-10-30)
The owner wants, first of all, to read the state (what goes to the display, and whether it is in standby). Changing the display comes later. Plan, without cutting anything:

Standby: a Pico GPIO (with a divider) on a signal that changes at power-on, such as POW/OF on the "TO SMPS PCB" connector or the VFD's power. Measure with the multimeter which one changes.
Display content: a "pass-through" FFC adapter (same number of lines and pitch) between the flat and the display board, with a pin header. Zero soldering, reversible.
Multimeter on each line (standby and on), then the logic analyzer (the same Pico as the sp103's) on the candidate lines.
Pico 2 W inside the amp, powered from D+5V (if it exists in standby), publishing /state as on the sp103.
Display ↔ main flat (from the photos, 2026-10-30; the owner counted 21): 21 lines, pitch ~1.0 mm (estimated; confirm by the width, ~21 mm). Buy: FFC extender board 21P 1.0 mm (or 24/30P with a 21-way connector soldered) with a 2.54 mm pin header + 21-way 1.0 mm flat ~10 cm, "same side".
Preferred path (2026-10-30), without an adapter: multimeter straight on the legs of the flat's connector, behind the display board; then solder thin wires only to GND, 5 V and the 3–4 signals (as on the SP103's LCD pins). The 21-way, 1.0 mm pass-through adapter with 2.54 mm holes is hard to find; it stays as an alternative.
Owner's decision (2026-10-30): buy an FFC 21P 1.0 mm extension (a bridge with two connectors) + 21P 1.0 mm flat ~10 cm "same side", and solder the Pico's wires to the extension, not to the Magnat's board. Confirm the flat's width (~21–22 mm).

Phases (owner's decision, 2026-10-30)
Phase 1: know the MR 780's state (what goes to the display, on/standby). Pico 2 W inside, listening to the flat through the FFC 21P extension. No Pi Zero inside for now.
Phase 2 (if possible): Raspberry Pi Zero 2W inside the amp, powered by the Magnat itself (no extra supply), receiving REC OUT through an internal connection, to reduce the cables outside. To investigate: the capacity of the 5 V rails (the main board's regulators and the transformer) and an I2S ADC (e.g. PCM1808), because the Zero has no analogue input.
Confirmed by the close-up photos (2026-10-30): 21 pins at both ends; 1.0 mm pitch (ruler: C97, 0805 = 2.0 mm). Buy the short flat in both types (Type A and Type B), because the contact side cannot be seen.
Writing (owner's idea, 2026-10-30): two FFC 21P extensions with the Pico in the middle. Only the VFD's data lines go through the Pico (it reads from the main board, writes to the display); the others go straight through jumpers. The VFD has its own supply, so any text can be written (without the SP103's limitation). It depends on knowing the protocol (phase 1) and perhaps on a level shifter if the display is 5 V. Buy two extensions.
A ready-made 21P 1.0 mm extension does not exist (2026-10-30). Build one: 2 universal boards "FFC/FPC 30P 0.5/1.0 mm to DIP 2.54 mm" + loose connectors "FFC 1.0mm 21 pin SMD" (solder aligned to pin 1) + 21 jumpers between the boards. Also works for phase 1 (each board has only one connector). Flats: 21P 1.0 mm ~10 cm, forward (A) and reverse (B).
Alternative chosen (2026-10-30): a 22P 1.0 mm extension (available ready-made) with the 21-way flat pushed against pin 1, the gap at pin 22 packed (plastic or Kapton), and a line-by-line continuity test before powering up. The flats stay 21P.
Pitch measured with tape (2026-10-30): 1.0 mm confirmed (21 lines, flat ~22 mm).
